Decisions (answer key)

D1
One question, many needs
Mr. D. comes to the front desk for help with his Medi-Cal renewal. While you work, he mentions a notice taped to his door last week. What do you do with what he just told you?
A. Finish the Medi-Cal form. The notice is a separate issue for another day
B. Listen, ask about the notice, and connect him to housing support as part of the same plan  ✓ Correct
C. Give him a phone number for legal aid and move on
Feedback: Correct: He came for one thing and told you another. Both belong to the same person and the same plan. Incorrect: Treating needs one at a time is how people fall through the gaps. AWD keeps them together.
Avoid: Splitting the person into separate requests, each handled by a different door, with no one holding the whole picture.
The St. Mary’s way: Listen first, see the whole person, and connect needs inside one coordinated plan.
D2
Where is she on the spectrum?
Ms. Linda Turner is 68. After a serious illness she fell behind on rent and lost her apartment. For the last few weeks she has been sleeping in her car. Which housing state describes where she is today?
A. Housing Insecure
B. Unhoused  ✓ Correct
C. Chronically Unhoused
Feedback: Correct: She has lost her home and is sleeping in a vehicle. She is Unhoused. It has been weeks, not 12 months, so she is not chronically unhoused. Incorrect: She no longer has housing, so she is past insecure. And chronic means 12 months or more. Today she is Unhoused.
Avoid: Waiting until someone reaches crisis before they count, or using a housing state as a label for who she is.
The St. Mary’s way: Name the state, not the person, and meet need before it becomes crisis.
D3
The lunch line is a door
Ms. R. is finishing lunch in the community center. She asks you, quietly, whether anyone here helps with housing. You do not work in housing. What is the No Wrong Door response?
A. Tell her to come back on a Housing Clinic day
B. Say housing is not your department
C. Welcome the question, and walk her to the right teammate or set up the connection yourself  ✓ Correct
Feedback: Correct: She walked through the lunch door. That is a real door. A warm handoff means she never has to start over. Incorrect: Every role is a front door. Sending her away, even politely, makes the door she chose the wrong one.
Avoid: Redirecting someone to the right place and leaving them to find it alone.
The St. Mary’s way: Welcome, connect, and make the warm handoff, whatever your seat.
